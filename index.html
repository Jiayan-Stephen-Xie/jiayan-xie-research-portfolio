TUBGCP2 function in prostate cancer cells

Huang Lab · Supervisor: Prof. Linfeng Huang

Does TUBGCP2 support survival and proliferation in human prostate cancer cells?

I use siRNA perturbation in PC-3 and DU145 cells to investigate this question. The project builds on my earlier RNAi work and places particular emphasis on verifying knockdown before interpreting a cellular phenotype.

My contribution

I designed multiple independent siRNAs, maintain the cell models, and optimize transfection and RT-qPCR conditions. I established controls for RNA delivery, the knockdown workflow and phenotypic response, with BIRC5 included as a benchmark.

Current status

Validating the assay system

Transfection efficiency and qPCR conditions are being optimized. The KIF11 positive control has shown reduced cell counts and morphological changes; no obvious phenotype has yet been observed in the TUBGCP2 groups.

Next stage

Confirm target knockdown, then evaluate viability, cell number and morphology. A dependency conclusion remains to be established.

Experimental design and controls +

Separating delivery from biological response

Multiple targeting sequences and complementary controls help identify whether a response reflects delivery, gene suppression or a downstream cellular effect.

Protein-level confirmation and functional assays, including CCK-8 viability measurement, cell counting and morphological imaging, form the follow-up plan. Mechanistic experiments will depend on the validated phenotype.

Building a pro-siRNA plasmid library

Huang Lab · Supervisor: Prof. Linfeng Huang

How can long hairpin RNA constructs support recombinant siRNA production in E. coli?

The 2025 SRS project focused on the molecular groundwork for RNAi-based cancer research: building gene-specific plasmids to support recombinant siRNA production. Our team constructed and verified a panel targeting 17 cancer-relevant genes, contributing components to the lab’s pro-siRNA library.

My contribution

I worked across primer design, high-fidelity PCR, sequential cloning into the p323 backbone, bacterial transformation, colony screening, plasmid extraction and sequence verification. I also helped troubleshoot restriction digestion and presented our work at the 2025 SRS showcase.

What the work established

The final report documents sequential assembly of sense and antisense fragments, followed by restriction mapping and Sanger sequencing to check the constructs. The team poster additionally reports siRNA-sized products of approximately 21 nt from representative library clones.

Cloning strategy, troubleshooting & next steps +
  1. 01

    Design & amplify

    Amplify 200–689 bp target regions as sense and antisense fragments, checking restriction sites and potential off-target sequences during primer design.

  2. 02

    Assemble sequentially

    Insert the sense fragment into p323 using SacI/XhoI, verify the intermediate, then add the antisense fragment using NotI/SalI.

  3. 03

    Screen & verify

    Use colony screening, three restriction-digestion combinations and Sanger sequencing to assess insert size, orientation and sequence integrity.

Learning from troubleshooting

The report records an improvement in digestion and gel readouts after extending digestion from 1.5 to 2 hours and doubling enzyme and DNA input. This was a practical lesson in evaluating cloning conditions and checking each intermediate before proceeding.

Next steps proposed in the report

The longer-term aim was to use the library for high-throughput RNAi screening in cancer cell models, with candidate follow-up using additional siRNA sequences and CRISPR. These were proposed extensions of the construct-building work.

2026 SRS project abstract

Functional Genomics in Insect Cell Models to Study Cellular Pathways

2026 SRS project abstract · Huang Lab, Duke Kunshan University

Understanding how genes shape cell behavior is essential for advancing basic biology. This project aimed to develop a functional genomics platform for studying gene function and cellular responses in insect cell models. We combined two complementary approaches: a pro-siRNA library screening workflow for identifying phenotypes associated with changes in cell viability and an eGFP reporter assay for evaluating RNA delivery and sequence-specific gene silencing.

First, we developed a modified screening workflow based on pro-siRNA technology, which provides a scalable method for generating RNA molecules that can reduce the activity of selected genes. More than thirty 96-well pro-siRNA library plates were prepared, and selected RNA samples were introduced into insect cells and analyzed using automated fluorescence imaging. At 72 hours after transfection, fluorescent markers were used to distinguish dead cells, viable cells, and cell nuclei, and the resulting images were quantified by high-content screening. The screen identified several candidate wells with elevated dead-to-live cell count ratios, indicating changes in cell viability.

Second, we developed a fluorescent reporter system to more directly evaluate RNA delivery and gene silencing. Two transfection reagents, PEI and Lipofectamine 3000, were compared for delivering messenger RNA encoding enhanced green fluorescent protein (eGFP). Flow cytometry showed that approximately 30% of cells were GFP-positive at both 24 and 48 hours following Lipofectamine 3000-mediated transfection. In a co-transfection experiment, cells received eGFP mRNA together with either an eGFP-targeting siRNA or a non-targeting control. Both high-content imaging and flow cytometry showed a lower GFP signal in the targeting-siRNA condition, providing a measurable readout of sequence-specific gene silencing.

Together, these complementary workflows enabled observation and quantification of changes in cell viability, RNA delivery, and sequence-specific reporter suppression in insect cell models.

2025 SRS final report · web edition

Construction and Verification of Long Hairpin RNA Plasmids for Recombinant siRNA Production in E. coli

Heyu Zhang, Jiayan Xie, Jiayi Xu, Kaige Sui, Muhan Wang
Supervisor: Prof. Linfeng Huang

2025 SRS final report · Web reading edition, adapted from the team report

Research rationale

The project aimed to contribute gene-specific plasmids to the Huang Lab’s pro-siRNA platform for recombinant siRNA production. The broader research goal was to build an RNAi screening resource for investigating cancer-relevant gene function, including genes involved in cell survival and proliferation. The work described in the report concentrated on constructing and verifying the molecular components required for that goal.

Cloning strategy

Target regions were selected downstream of the start codon, with the report describing insert lengths of 200–689 bp. Primer design considered restriction sites and potential off-target sequences. High-fidelity PCR generated separate sense and antisense fragments for sequential assembly into the p323 plasmid backbone.

The sense fragment was inserted using SacI/XhoI, followed by bacterial transformation and screening of intermediate constructs. The antisense fragment was then inserted using NotI/SalI. Plasmid extraction, colony screening and restriction analysis were used during assembly. Final quality control combined three restriction-digestion combinations—SacI/XhoI, SalI/NotI and SacI/NotI—with Sanger sequencing.

Reported results

The team reported a panel targeting 17 cancer-relevant genes. PCR products and restriction patterns were assessed against expected fragment sizes, while sequencing supported assessment of insert orientation and sequence integrity. These results supported the construction of plasmid components for the larger pro-siRNA library.

The summer report’s central outcome was construct validation. The separate team poster provides additional RNA-product observations from representative clones, including siRNA-sized products of approximately 21 nt.

Experimental troubleshooting

The report describes extending restriction digestion from 1.5 to 2 hours and doubling enzyme and DNA input. The team observed improved digestion, gel-band visibility and reproducibility under the revised conditions. This optimization formed part of the practical work of establishing and checking the cloning workflow.

Scope and proposed continuation

The constructed plasmids represented part of a larger project. The report proposed using a more complete library for high-throughput RNAi screening in cancer cell models, including liver cancer models, followed by candidate validation with additional siRNA sequences and CRISPR. These activities were presented as future work in the report.

My contribution within the team

My contribution included primer design, high-fidelity PCR, sequential cloning, bacterial transformation, colony screening, plasmid extraction, sequence verification and troubleshooting. I also presented the work at the 2025 SRS showcase. The 17-gene panel and associated experimental results were collaborative outcomes.

The contribution statement follows my CV and research account. This web edition summarizes the team report and preserves its author order.
